01Project title

Tender Risk ML — predicting problematic public procurements in Kazakhstan from open procurement data

02Problem description

Kazakhstan's public procurement system (goszakup.gov.kz) processes roughly 1.8 million procurements per year — 12.9 million in total as of September 2026, with over 40 million supplier bids — spending a significant share of the state budget.

Every procurement, lot, bid and contract is published through the open OWS v3 GraphQL API. Oversight bodies, journalists and auditors, however, work by hand: they can inspect a few hundred procurements a year, while the signs of a problematic procurement — a single bidder, an implausible price discount, a competitive procedure that “fails” and is re-awarded to a single source, a supplier with a history of terminated contracts — are scattered across several linked tables and are invisible at the level of one record.

The practical consequence is that a large share of contracts end badly. In a sample of FY2024 contracts, about one in five was terminated or never concluded, and about 7% of procurements were cancelled after publication. Each such case means a repeated procedure, delayed goods or services, and often wasted budget. Detecting the procurements likely to fail before they fail would let a controlling body concentrate its limited attention where it matters.

An earlier engineering project by the author (Tender Radar) approached this with 31 hand-written risk rules. Rules are transparent but arbitrary: their thresholds were chosen by intuition, and there was no way to measure whether a “risky” procurement actually turned out badly. This project replaces intuition with a supervised model trained on observed outcomes, using the rules only as candidate features.

03Project goal

Build and evaluate a machine-learning classifier that, using only information available at the time a procurement is published and awarded, predicts whether the procurement will have a problematic outcome — defined as the resulting contract being terminated or never concluded — and identify which characteristics of a procurement are most predictive of that outcome.

04Project objectives

Dataset construction A1

Collect a linked snapshot of procurements published in Q1 2024 from the OWS v3 API — procurements, lots, bids, contracts and the register of unreliable suppliers — and publish it as a reproducible, documented dataset (collection script + data dictionary).

Data preparation and EDA A2

Clean and normalise the data (missing values, duplicates, currency/date formats, text normalisation for Russian/Kazakh lot names), explore distributions and class balance, and define the target rigorously with a leakage-safe time-based train/validation/test split.

Feature engineering A2

Derive tabular features (price, number of bidders, discount, trade method, customer and supplier history, the 31 Tender Radar indicators) and text features (TF-IDF over lot names and descriptions).

Baseline modelling and evaluation Midterm

Train and compare classical models (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, gradient boosting) on tabular + TF-IDF features; report ROC-AUC, PR-AUC and F1 on the held-out test period, with an interpretation of feature importance.

Model improvement A3

Optimise hyperparameters of the best classical model and add a text branch by fine-tuning a pre-trained multilingual transformer (transfer learning from xlm-roberta-base / rubert-tiny2) on lot descriptions; compare with, and ensemble into, the tabular model.

05Type of AI task

✓ Classification ＋ NLP component Regression Clustering Computer vision Generative AI

Binary classification (supervised) on tabular data, with an NLP component: lot names and descriptions are free text in Russian and Kazakh, vectorised with TF-IDF for the baseline and, in the fine-tuning stage, encoded by a pre-trained transformer. Because positive cases are the minority (~20%), evaluation focuses on ROC-AUC, PR-AUC and F1 rather than accuracy.

06Dataset

6.1 Source and access

  • Source: OWS v3 GraphQL API, ows.goszakup.gov.kz/v3/graphql — the official open-data interface of the public procurement portal. Access via a personal API token (valid until 13 Nov 2026). The data is public.
  • Scope: all procurements with publishDate in 2024-01-01 … 2024-03-31 — 630,762 procurements, about 35% of the 1,818,344 FY2024 procurements — and every linked record. Q1 2024 is old enough that every contract has had more than two years to reach a final status, so the outcome label is settled.
  • Collection: src/collect.py — resumable limit/after pagination, stored as JSONL and Parquet. The snapshot is published as a GitHub release asset; the script reproduces it from scratch.

Why an observed outcome rather than the rule-based risk score: a model trained on labels produced by hand-written rules only learns those rules. Contract termination is a real, independently recorded event, so the model's quality can be measured honestly and the rules can be tested as features.

6.4 Leakage control

Only fields known at publication/award time enter the feature set: procurement and lot attributes, bids, winner identity, and the history of the customer and the supplier strictly before the procurement's publish date. Contract execution fields (faktSum, faktExecDate, status) are used for the label only. The split is by time — train on January–February, validate/test on March 2024 — so that no future information leaks into training.

6.5 Feature groups (preliminary)

Procurement

Trade method, subject type, total sum, number of lots, planned duration, repeat flag, single-organiser flag, construction / light-industry flags, region (KATO).

Competition

Number of bidders, bids per lot, winner's discount, dumping flag, price dispersion among bids.

Text

TF-IDF (word + character n-grams) of lot names and descriptions; transformer embeddings in A3.

Supplier / customer history

Computed strictly before the publish date: number of prior contracts, share terminated, share of wins with the same customer, presence in RNU.

Rule indicators

The 31 Tender Radar indicators, re-implemented in pandas and used as input features rather than as labels.
